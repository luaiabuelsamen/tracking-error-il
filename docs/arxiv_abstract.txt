The ACT configuration studied here exposes one timestep of measured joint positions to the policy. We show that appending the servo tracking error, the difference between the last applied position command and the current measured position, changes what a policy can learn at a 50-demonstration budget. On a physical SO-101, ACT policies with tracking error place the object in 23 of 40 paired trials against 6 of 40 for position-only policies (post-hoc stratified exact test p=0.0005), after a fixed demonstration replay, in both completed training seeds (9 vs 5 and 14 vs 1). In simulation over five seeds, placement rises from 6.2% to 21.8%, and a position-history control recovers most of that gain, consistent with a benefit from temporal information without establishing a preferred representation. The signal requires no added sensor: it is reconstructible from logs with aligned commands and positions, and it keeps varying where the servo's load register saturates at a configured torque limit in 16.5% of demonstration frames. Across 546 archived teleoperation episodes, a preregistered prediction that larger tracking error at grasp onset accompanies a gripper-retention proxy fails (pooled d=-0.39). The study motivates reporting observation history, applied-command semantics, and actuator limits as experimental choices.
